# Project Ironwright — Release Playtest Protocol

This protocol covers the external human validation still required after automated release certification. It is designed around the game’s defining risks: long save integrity, continuous pressure, anti-chore autonomy, physical remote operations and the emotional contrast between opening helplessness and late machine sovereignty.

## Test cohorts

Use at least four cohorts:

1. players familiar with survival games but not RTS games;
2. players familiar with automation and strategy games;
3. players who abandoned large-base games because of management fatigue;
4. accessibility-focused players using controller, enlarged text, reduced motion, reduced flashes, sound captions or hold/toggle alternatives.

Each cohort should include first-time players who have not read the design documents.

## Session structure

### Opening test — 60 to 120 minutes

Measure whether players:

- notice the marked wreck immediately;
- understand Bulwark dependence;
- understand that salvage and fabrication disable attack;
- interpret noise as a causal risk rather than a random punishment;
- construct the first Scrapper without external instruction;
- recognize the first autonomous salvage as work permanently removed;
- understand why the North Ruins group moves at shared pace.

A successful opening is tense and legible. A player should not fail because a menu was clipped, a target was invisible, or the current interaction was ambiguous.

### Foothold test — 4 to 8 hours

Measure:

- whether the player understands the three macro focuses;
- whether local automation reduces actions per minute;
- whether robot losses are meaningful but recoverable;
- whether continuous pressure produces decisions rather than notification fatigue;
- whether the first outpost feels like a strategic commitment rather than a second base to administer;
- whether players can follow and understand remote construction and hauling.

### Mid-run test — 15 to 30 hours

Measure:

- time spent making strategic choices versus servicing routine systems;
- number of manual interventions per in-game day;
- frequency of alerts requiring no meaningful decision;
- whether outpost count increases workload;
- whether physical operations remain understandable at distance;
- whether region identity and ecology change route and technology choices;
- whether the player can recover from a serious setback without grinding repetitive tasks.

### Endurance test — 30 to 100 hours

Measure:

- save/load reliability across updates and crashes;
- size and load time of long saves;
- active/reduced-detail equivalence;
- performance at maximum expected machine and organic populations;
- whether late-game UI becomes calmer;
- whether autonomous replacement, repair, hauling and rebuilding work without babysitting;
- whether the Heartforge remains strategically vulnerable;
- whether the endgame prerequisites are legible;
- whether Severance and Containment both produce a credible culmination.

## Difficulty profiles

Test Story, Survival and Brutal separately.

### Story

The player should experience the full systemic arc with wider recovery margins. It must not remove the Bulwark dependency, physical operations or meaningful consequences.

### Survival

This is the intended first-victory profile. Target repeated setbacks and several failed worlds before victory for players engaging without spoilers, while ensuring each failure has an understandable cause.

### Brutal

This should demand stronger planning and loss acceptance without requiring faster clicking, unit micro-management or exploitative grinding.

## Anti-chore measures

Record these metrics at 2, 10, 25 and 50 hours:

- deliberate strategic authorizations per hour;
- routine repair or replacement inputs per hour;
- individual robot selections per hour, expected to remain zero;
- alerts dismissed without a decision;
- time spent in management interfaces;
- number of active robots and outposts;
- player-reported mental load;
- percentage of play time observing or intervening in meaningful events.

The milestone fails if routine inputs rise approximately in proportion to robot or outpost count.

## Failure analysis

After every collapse, ask the player to state the perceived cause before showing the game’s report. Compare that answer with recorded state:

- first constrained resource;
- decisive ecological pressure source;
- operation or route loss;
- outpost destruction and rebuild readiness;
- machine-composition shortage;
- ignored warning;
- technology commitment;
- reserve depletion;
- final-protocol preparation.

If the player and report disagree substantially, improve telegraphing before increasing difficulty.

## Accessibility protocol

Validate at minimum:

- controller-only play from title to victory;
- 140% text scaling at 1280×720;
- high-contrast mode in all major interfaces;
- reduced-motion mode during combat, secondary animation and final protocol;
- reduced-flash mode during impacts and victory;
- sound captions with music and effects muted independently;
- toggle interaction mode for salvage and fabrication;
- color-independent role and threat recognition.

Professional accessibility review and testing with affected players are required before claiming comprehensive accessibility compliance.

## Hardware matrix

Cover:

- integrated graphics near the minimum target;
- representative mid-range hardware;
- high-refresh systems;
- AMD, Intel and NVIDIA graphics where possible;
- Windows 10/11 and at least two current Linux distributions;
- 16:9, 16:10, ultrawide and constrained windowed resolutions;
- common Xbox-layout and PlayStation-layout controllers.

Capture average frame rate, one-percent lows, memory use, save/load times and any simulation-budget adaptation.

## Release decision

A final retail 1.0 should require:

- no known save corruption path;
- successful recovery from intentionally interrupted saves;
- stable completion of multiple long runs;
- no major inaccessible interface at supported resolutions;
- acceptable performance on the minimum target;
- no progression dead end;
- no dominant strategy that invalidates the main decision paths;
- no evidence that player workload scales linearly with game size;
- professional language, accessibility, legal and store review completed.
