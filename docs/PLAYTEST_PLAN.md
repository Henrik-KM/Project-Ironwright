# Playtest Plan

## 1. Core questions

Every playtest should answer one or more of these questions:

1. Is the early game frightening without relying on many enemies?
2. Does the Heartforge feel like home?
3. Does each autonomy unlock permanently reduce work?
4. Can the player trust machines without configuring them?
5. Does pressure feel continuous and causal rather than wave-based?
6. Does the game remain centred on base defence during excursions?
7. Does player workload remain stable as the machine population grows?
8. Does a failed world teach a strategic lesson?

---

## 2. First Light test

### Setup

- one damaged Heartforge;
- one Scrapling;
- one Veilstalker;
- one nearby wreck;
- darkness and a narrow light radius;
- 15–25 minute session.

### Observe

- time before player first leaves light;
- maximum distance travelled;
- whether player notices the stalker before attack;
- whether player retreats voluntarily;
- whether the Scrapling is perceived as useful, vulnerable, or annoying;
- emotional response upon returning home;
- management actions attempted or expected.

### Success indicators

- player expresses caution without tutorial text demanding caution;
- player understands the immediate goal;
- one creature creates tension;
- no one asks where the base-building menu is during the opening;
- death feels attributable to risk rather than surprise.

---

## 3. First Delegation test

### Setup

The player performs one routine task several times, then unlocks machine autonomy for that task.

### Observe

- whether the player notices the machine performing it;
- whether the player continues trying to perform it manually;
- whether the player understands why the machine is acting;
- whether the unlock feels more valuable than a numerical upgrade;
- whether a new menu or monitoring habit appears.

### Success indicators

- manual repetitions drop to nearly zero;
- player describes relief or increased trust;
- no configuration is required;
- the machine succeeds under ordinary variation.

---

## 4. Continuous-pressure test

### Setup

Run 30 minutes with no scheduled wave. Use roaming creatures, scavenging, stalking, environmental cues, and one possible causal incident.

### Observe

- periods of tension without direct combat;
- frequency of player interruption;
- whether the player can infer changing risk;
- whether routine defence feels alive;
- whether the player becomes bored during calm periods;
- whether pressure feels arbitrary.

### Success indicators

- the player reports feeling under pressure despite no countdown;
- routine incidents do not require repeated commands;
- the player can name at least one environmental warning;
- the rare incident has an understandable cause.

---

## 5. Workload scaling test

### Setup

Prepare equivalent scenarios with:

- 10 machines;
- 50 machines;
- 100 machines.

Scale routine activity and threat proportionally.

### Metrics

- decisions requiring confirmation per minute;
- alerts per minute;
- time in menus;
- manual unit selections;
- repeated repair or build interactions;
- number of unexplained machine failures;
- subjective mental workload.

### Pass condition

The 100-machine scenario should not require materially more routine input than the 10-machine scenario. Increased attention is acceptable only during a genuinely larger strategic crisis.

---

## 6. Expedition test

### Setup

Present one machine-proposed expedition with a meaningful base-defence trade-off.

### Observe

- information needed before authorisation;
- whether players ask to select units or routes;
- trust in risk estimate;
- willingness to accompany versus delegate;
- clarity of debrief;
- response to losses.

### Success indicators

- player can decide from a short proposal;
- execution is understandable without manual control;
- losses feel connected to known risk;
- the base remains the reason the expedition matters.

---

## 7. Long-world diary test

For tests longer than five hours, participants keep a short diary after each session:

- What is the main strategic problem in your world?
- What decision are you postponing?
- What do your machines now handle that they could not handle earlier?
- What currently frightens you outside the Heartforge?
- What task feels repetitive?
- What would make you stop this run?

On return after two or more days, ask whether the recap restored context without opening management screens.

---

## 8. Failure review

After defeat, ask the player to explain the causal chain before showing the game report. Compare their explanation with telemetry.

Success means:

- player and report agree on the main cause;
- the player identifies at least one earlier decision that mattered;
- the player wants to try a different strategy rather than merely grind bonuses;
- the loss was not caused by neglected routine maintenance.

---

## 9. Stop conditions

Pause production expansion if playtests show any of the following:

- late-game menus become denser than early-game menus;
- players repeatedly select individual machines;
- players describe repair, placement, or resupply as their main activity;
- scheduled attacks are needed to make the game tense;
- players feel safe clearing outward rather than afraid of hostile space;
- machine autonomy fails without continuous supervision;
- the base stops feeling central;
- organic enemies read as a reskinned army rather than an ecology.
